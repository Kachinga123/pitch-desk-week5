# SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM

## **Fresh Milk Is Produced, But Much of Its Value Is Lost**

Pastoralist households in Turkana produce goat and sheep milk, but face major challenges:

* Limited access to reliable milk collection points.
* High temperatures and long distances make milk spoil quickly.
* Poor preservation and storage lead to milk spoilage and income losses.
* Producers often have limited access to stable and profitable markets.
* Seasonal milk availability makes consistent supply difficult.
* Children and families can miss out on the nutritional value of milk.

**The result:** wasted milk, lost household income, and missed nutrition opportunities.

---

# SLIDE 3 — OUR SOLUTION

## **A Reliable Milk Collection & Preservation Network**

**Turkana MilkLink** will establish community-based milk collection points where pastoralist producers can bring fresh goat and sheep milk.

We will:

* Collect milk from local pastoralist producers.
* Test and record milk quality.
* Use appropriate cooling and preservation methods.
* Aggregate milk to create a reliable supply.
* Connect producers with reliable buyers and markets.
* Provide basic training on hygienic milking, handling and storage.

### **Our goal**

**Reduce milk losses while increasing the value and market access of pastoralist milk.**

# SLIDE 5 — MARKET OPPORTUNITY

## **A Large and Underserved Pastoralist Market**

Turkana has a large pastoralist population and livestock is central to household livelihoods.

This creates an opportunity to build a local milk value chain that connects:

**Pastoralist producers → Collection points → Preservation → Buyers → Consumers**

### **Market opportunity**

* Large numbers of households depend on livestock.
* Goat and sheep milk are already part of pastoralist livelihoods.
* Demand exists for safe, fresh and conveniently available milk.
* Better aggregation can make small volumes commercially valuable.
* Improved preservation can extend the time available to reach markets.

### **Our opportunity**

Start with a focused community-level model and scale through additional collection centres.

# SLIDE 6 — BUSINESS MODEL

## **How Turkana MilkLink Makes Money**

Our revenue will come primarily from the milk value chain.

### **1. Milk sales**

Purchase/collect milk from producers and sell aggregated milk to consumers and institutional or commercial buyers.

### **2. Value-added products**

As the business grows, introduce suitable preserved or processed milk products.

### **3. Collection services**

Provide reliable milk aggregation and handling services for larger buyers.

### **Producer benefit**

Producers receive a more reliable market and the opportunity to earn better value from milk that might otherwise spoil.

### **Business principle**

**Buy locally → preserve safely → aggregate efficiently → sell reliably.**

# SLIDE 7 — COMPETITION & ALTERNATIVES

## **What Customers Use Today**

Our customers currently rely on several alternatives:

* Selling milk directly to neighbours or local traders.
* Consuming milk within the household.
* Transporting milk long distances without adequate cooling.
* Traditional preservation methods.
* Allowing excess milk to spoil when there is no nearby buyer.

### **The gap**

Most alternatives do not combine:

**Local collection + quality handling + preservation + market connection**

in one community-focused system.
